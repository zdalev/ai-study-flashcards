English: Grammar and Literature Basics

PARTS OF SPEECH

A noun names a person, place, thing or idea. A verb describes an action or a state. An adjective
describes a noun, and an adverb describes a verb, an adjective or another adverb. A pronoun, such as
"she" or "they", takes the place of a noun. A preposition, such as "in" or "under", shows the
relationship between a noun and another word in the sentence.

TENSES AND SENTENCES

The simple past tense describes an action that was completed in the past, for example "She walked
home". The present perfect tense is formed with "have" or "has" plus the past participle, as in "I
have finished my homework". A sentence needs at least a subject and a verb. A complex sentence has a
main clause and at least one subordinate clause, which cannot stand alone.

LITERARY TERMS

A metaphor says that one thing is another thing, for example "Time is a thief". A simile compares
two things using "like" or "as". Personification gives human qualities to something that is not
human. The theme of a story is its central message or idea. The narrator is the voice that tells the
story; in first-person narration the narrator is a character who says "I".
